AI Adoption Lead — Artificial Intelligence Office · Job Reference 14012

I've spent fifteen years building systems that actually work — from neural networks predicting grain quality on a factory floor to locally-deployed LLMs quietly extracting structured data from expense receipts inside a corporate perimeter. The engineering part I know well. The moment that interests me more, though, is when someone who's never touched a line of code opens a tool and says "this changes how we work." That last metre — from working AI to adopted AI — is the problem worth solving, and it's the problem the ECB's AI Office exists to tackle.

The timing matters. The ECB is standing up its AI Office at exactly the moment when AI has crossed from experimental into genuinely useful — but most institutions are still figuring out what that means for them in practice. A newly formed office means the opportunity to shape how adoption happens, not inherit someone else's half-finished framework. For an institution whose decisions touch the economic lives of 350 million people, getting that right is worth doing carefully and with real technical grounding — not just strategic enthusiasm.

At Brainlab, my role has effectively been an internal AI and automation consultancy for a global organisation of 3,000+ people. Departments across HR, Finance, IT and Accounting bring problems. My job is to figure out which are genuinely solvable with AI or automation, which would create more complexity than they remove, and how to deliver the ones worth pursuing — end to end, with governance built in from the start. The locally-hosted LLM pipeline I built for CO2 emissions tracking wasn't just an engineering preference. It was a deliberate institutional decision: sensitive financial data stays inside the perimeter. That's not a technical footnote — it's the governance conversation at the centre of responsible AI adoption.

The technical depth is real: machine learning for industrial food quality analysis at TecnoCientífica, computer vision and sensor fusion from the Udacity self-driving programme, autonomous AI-driven website generation as a live personal project. I can hold a genuine conversation with an AI research team without needing things translated. I can also walk a CFO through what a model actually does — and more importantly, what it doesn't — in plain language. I've done both, repeatedly, in the same week.
